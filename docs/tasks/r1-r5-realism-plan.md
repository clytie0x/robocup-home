## R1 Sensor boundary

1. Separate controller and sensor types from world truth and from the scorer. The controller does not receive car coordinates or the full list of unseen objects. World execution may use truth to enforce collisions, reach, and custody.
2. Follow, approach, bag selection, and manipulation decisions consume sensor-limited data. The handoff cue is a human gesture that is visible in range. It is not generated by equality with the true car coordinates. The scenario may script human intentions. It does not hand the controller a terminal answer.
3. The evaluator checks the actual car, custody, geometry, and time. A task-success flag is not enough.

Exit. Hold a sensor history fixed and swap the true car position. Controller actions stay identical. An out-of-range gesture is unusable. A cue at the wrong place may stop the robot and must fail the actual main outcome. An unseen object is absent from the sensor view and still present in truth. One in-range gesture at the real car still passes the main outcome. Existing section 3.1 evidence stays honest.

## R2 Stamps and one synthetic profile

1. Capture and delivery stamps use the existing sim clock. A missing sample stays missing. There is no fallback to truth.
2. Delays are 0 ms, 200 ms, and 1000 ms. Cases include one missing sample, a stale sample, and a reversed sample. Stop and request-fresh behavior stays bounded.
3. One bounded deterministic positional perturbation profile is explicitly synthetic and unmeasured. The zero profile is the control.

The current base pose is proprioception. Follow, approach, and accompany use that pose as the motion origin. The posted sample still supplies the people, bodies, bags, pointing ray, and cue. The controller does not replace those with a fresh truth list or the car. A planned step longer than the base can take requests a fresh sample, advances the authoritative clock, and does not keep that sample.

Exit. The zero-profile trace matches the baseline. A reversed sample is rejected atomically and does not change pose or custody. Missing data does not reveal truth. The episode clock enforces 5 minutes independently of stale sample stamps.

## R3 Causal motion and manipulation

1. The human walks on after a bounded wait. There is no teleport. Visibility and distance cause continue, stop, or lost.
2. A dynamic obstacle, or one that blocks the approach, makes the robot avoid or stop for a cause the run can name. A sensor miss never disables the physical collision guard.
3. An out-of-reach grasp fails and a blocked handoff fails, with custody unchanged in the failure direction the case names. A drop comes from world custody, not from a flag alone. Legacy faults stay explicit and separate.

Exit. Normal follow, natural loss, blocked approach, failed grasp, failed handoff, and drop controls are reproducible. Removing the cause changes the outcome. This milestone does not invent collision-to-drop physics.

## R4 Sixteen-scenario manifest

1. A fixed manifest of 16 scenarios locks the expected outcome before the runs. The set covers normal, wrong cue, two delays, a missing person, a missing obstacle, a reversed stamp, position error, natural loss, blocked approach, grasp, handoff, drop, crowd 3 versus 4, and unmassaged geometry. Not every case has to succeed.
2. Seed and profile replay, candidate-order invariance, and held-out seeds that were not tuned to pass.
3. The independent evaluator reads truth, collision, custody, and time. It does not read the planner.

Exit. One command fills a denominator of 16 plus the metamorphic and negative controls. The report lists failures, `not-run`, and reasons. The old 11 `not-run` rows stay unchanged. There is no field total.

## Gates

Each milestone uses a failing behavioral test before production code, then the full regression commands. CI runs on the exact final head. An independent reviewer reports before the coordinator merges. The program ends with a multi-challenger interrogate: each finding is reproduced independently, then the fixed final head is checked again. CI is not that audit.

## Residual limitations

These limits stay after R1. They are not field acceptance.

- A visible bag inside the printed 2 m of the operator is in the sensor view only while that operator is already inside the robot sensor. That disk is a labeled setup assumption. It is not a measured camera and not a halo around an unseen person.
- The model has no occlusion, no pixels, and no contact-force calibration.
- The operator route is scripted by the scenario. A loss walk is a sequence of steps of at most 200 mm on the sim clock. The scenario may use the car. The controller does not.
- `collision_fault`, `grasp_fault`, and `handoff_fault` are legacy diagnostic injections. A sensor hide does not remove a body from the slide. A collision does not invent a drop.
- The positional profile is a synthetic unmeasured shift of 8 mm on sensed people, bodies, and bags. The zero shift is the control. It is not a calibration. The robot base pose in the sample is not shifted.
- The motion origin is the live base. A delayed sample does not carry its captured base pose into the step. Exteroceptive points stay on that sample.
- Component tests may still leave the world clock and the trip stamp apart. The episode scorer requires them to match.
- `CML-BAG-DEF`, the three hardware rows, and the seven open scoring rows stay `not-run`. The sheet does not award a run total.
- A collision does not invent a drop.
